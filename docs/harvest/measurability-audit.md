## Measurability Audit: AI Takeoff Scoreboard Metrics

**METRIC 1: Combined hyperscaler AI capex (Microsoft/Google/Amazon/Meta, annualized)**
SOURCE: Primary — each company's SEC 10-Q/10-K, e.g. Microsoft's FY26 Q2 10-Q at https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526027207/msft-20251231.htm (cash flow statement, "Additions to property and equipment" line); full filing index at https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&amp;CIK=0000789019&amp;type=10-Q; company IR pages, e.g. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q3/cash-flows. Secondary/aggregated — https://valueaddvc.com/ai-spending (free dashboard combining the four companies' guidance, currently showing ~$725B combined 2026 total: Amazon ~$200B, Google $175–185B, Meta $115–145B, Microsoft ~$120–190B depending on source vintage). Amazon's actual Q1 2026 cash capex ($43.2B, per earnings call transcripts on Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance) corroborates the guidance trajectory.
CADENCE: Quarterly (10-Q filings + earnings calls); third-party trackers refresh irregularly, "regularly" per site but no fixed schedule.
COST: Free (SEC EDGAR and company IR are free; valueaddvc.com dashboard free).
CAVEATS: 10-Q capex line is total company capex (buildings, servers, leases, non-AI equipment), not AI-specific — real ambiguity flagged by the task itself. Companies don't break out "AI capex" cleanly in filings; analysts back it out from commentary (e.g., Microsoft disclosed ~57% of a $68B PP&amp;E addition was IT equipment/GPUs, per Epoch AI's https://epoch.ai/data-insights/microsoft-ppe-breakdown). Full-year "guidance" figures are forward-looking management commentary, not audited numbers, and get revised each quarter. Third-party aggregator trackers (valueaddvc, Futurum, tech-insider) don't cite exact filing paragraphs, so cross-check against primary 10-Qs before feeding a scoreboard.
VERDICT: feedable with effort (primary quarterly total-capex numbers are solid; true "AI-specific" capex requires estimation/triangulation, not a clean line item).

**METRIC 2: Largest known training run (FLOP)**
SOURCE: Epoch AI notable models database — https://epoch.ai/data/notable-ai-models (interactive DB with explicit "Training compute (FLOP)" axis/column, CSV downloadable under CC-BY) and https://epoch.ai/data-insights/models-over-1e25-flop (directly fetched: confirms Grok-3 tops the list at 4.6e26 FLOP, ahead of Claude Opus 4 at 1.5e26, GPT-4.5 at 6.4e25). Methodology page: https://epoch.ai/data/ai-models-documentation/estimation.
CADENCE: Continuously updated as new models are released/estimated; data-insight pages get periodic refresh snapshots.
COST: Free, open license (CC-BY).
CAVEATS: Values are a mix of directly-reported (from papers/technical reports) and Epoch's own estimates from architecture/hardware/duration — Epoch explicitly tags confidence as "Confident" (±3x), "Likely" (±10x), or "Speculative" (±30x), so precision varies a lot by model. Frontier labs (OpenAI, Google, xAI) frequently don't publish exact compute, so top-of-leaderboard entries are often estimates, not confirmed figures — genuinely apples-to-oranges in certainty even though the numbers sit in the same column.
VERDICT: feedable now (with the caveat that "largest known" really means "largest Epoch-estimated," not audited).

**METRIC 3: Largest operational AI cluster/campus power (MW–GW), announced vs. energized**
SOURCE: SemiAnalysis Datacenter Industry Model (paywalled institutional product) — https://semianalysis.com/datacenter-industry-model/ (confirmed via direct fetch: tracks 5,000+ datacenters by hyperscaler/site/cluster with self-build and leased MW, covers Microsoft, AWS, Meta, Google, Oracle, CoreWeave, Tesla, xAI). Free companion newsletter analysis with real figures — https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/stop-saying-half-of-2026-us-datacenter (confirmed: distinguishes "announced/pre-construction" from under-construction/operational; cites 410GW ERCOT interconnection requests, 311GW "phantom" demand, vs. only ~12GW actual expected 2026 US capacity and ~5GW under construction). Company-specific: xAI Colossus 2 — 350–400MW operational mid-2025, targeting 1GW mid-2026 (per SemiAnalysis newsletter and Data Center Dynamics coverage); Microsoft Fairwater — 2GW+ total planned across sites (NextBigFuture, Microsoft announcements).
CADENCE: SemiAnalysis newsletter posts multiple times/month; full industry model reportedly updated continuously (satellite imagery + permits). No single authoritative real-time public tracker.
COST: Newsletter articles mostly paywalled (some free); full Datacenter Industry Model is a paid institutional subscription, price not publicly listed (contact sales).
CAVEATS: "Announced" vs. "operational/energized" is the single biggest measurement gap in this whole cluster — companies and press routinely conflate the two (hence SemiAnalysis's "stop saying half is canceled" pushback piece). No neutral, free, standardized tracker distinguishes them at cluster-level; you're stitching together paywalled analyst estimates, company press releases (self-reported, PR-optimized), and outlet reporting (DCD, Neowin) of uneven rigor.
VERDICT: feedable with effort (real numbers exist but are scattered across paywalled/PR sources with no single free canonical tracker; requires triangulation and manual judgment calls on "operational" definition).

**METRIC 4: Global/US datacenter power demand and forecasts (GW)**
SOURCE: IEA "Electricity 2025" report — https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2025/demand and full PDF https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/7c671ef6-2947-4e87-beea-af0e1288e1d7/Electricity2025.pdf; IEA "Energy and AI" report — https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai; IEA news update — https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions. Search-verified specific figures (direct site fetch was blocked by Cloudflare bot-protection on iea.org, so these are corroborated via multiple independent search snippets quoting the IEA pages directly, not just inferred): global data center electricity demand 485 TWh (2025) → 950 TWh (2030); US data center demand 31 GW (2025) → 66 GW (2027); data center electricity demand grew 17% in 2025 vs. 3% overall global growth. LBNL "United States Data Center Energy Usage Report" is the other standard citation but wasn't independently re-verified this session.
CADENCE: IEA Electricity report is annual (with a "Mid-Year Update"); Energy and AI report was a one-off major report (April 2025) with periodic follow-up analysis pieces.
COST: Free to read online (PDF free); IEA's underlying data explorer/some datasets require registration but core report content is open.
CAVEATS: iea.org is bot-gated (Cloudflare), so automated/programmatic re-fetching for a live scoreboard will need a real browser or manual re-check each cycle, not a simple scraper. Definitions of "data center power demand" vary (IT critical load vs. total facility power including cooling/PUE overhead), and IEA/LBNL/McKinsey/Goldman forecasts use different base years and scenarios (Base Case vs. High/AI-accelerated case), so cross-source comparisons need explicit scenario labeling.
VERDICT: feedable now (numbers are real, cited, and free), but note bot-blocking as an operational friction for automated refresh.

**METRIC 5: Grid interconnection queue times and transformer/turbine lead times**
SOURCE: LBNL "Queued Up" series — landing page https://emp.lbl.gov/queues, most recent edition (2026 Edition, covering data through end of 2025) with PDF at https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/queued_up_2025_edition_12.15.2025.pdf plus a downloadable Excel dataset. Direct fetch of both emp.lbl.gov and the PDF was blocked by Cloudflare bot protection this session, but content and specific figures were corroborated via independent secondary sources (OSTI.gov mirror at https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2335720, interconnectionfyi.substack.com, loopbacksystems.com) quoting LBNL directly: median interconnection-request-to-commercial-operation duration is now &gt;5 years for 2025-completed projects (up from &lt;2 years for 2000–2007 projects), aggregating &gt;50 grid operators (~97–98% of US installed capacity). No equivalent free, standardized public tracker was found specifically for large power transformer or gas turbine lead times — that data lives in scattered industry/trade reports (S&amp;P Global Commodity Insights, DOE analyses) rather than one citable dataset.
CADENCE: LBNL publishes "Queued Up" annually (each edition covers data through the end of the prior calendar year); Excel dataset updated alongside each report.
COST: Free, public (LBNL/DOE-funded research).
CAVEATS: emp.lbl.gov is Cloudflare-protected against automated access, which will complicate a live/automated scoreboard feed (manual download or an approved scraping path needed). Queue-time data is generator-interconnection-request-based, not datacenter-load-interconnection-based — it's a reasonable proxy for grid bottleneck severity but doesn't directly measure datacenter hookup times, which is the thing that actually matters for AI buildout timelines. Transformer/turbine lead times have no single authoritative free public source at all — this sub-metric would need to rely on paywalled industry reports or periodic DOE/trade-press mentions (weakest link in this whole cluster).
VERDICT: feedable with effort for interconnection queue times (real, free, annual data exists but access is bot-gated); no reliable public source for transformer/turbine lead times specifically.

**Overall note:** This cluster's biggest common weakness isn't data existence — real, citable numbers exist for every metric — it's granularity and access friction: capex is total-not-AI-specific, cluster power is announced-vs-operational-conflated, and both SEC EDGAR and the highest-quality energy sources (IEA, LBNL) are bot-gated against the kind of automated refresh a live scoreboard would want, so a "citable" pipeline here means periodic manual verification against primary filings/reports rather than a clean API feed.

## Cluster: Metrics 6, 7, 8, 13, 18 (revenue / economics)

METRIC 6: Frontier lab revenue run-rates (OpenAI, Anthropic)
SOURCE: Anthropic's own blog confirms figures directly — https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute states verbatim: "Our run-rate revenue has now surpassed $30 billion—up from approximately $9 billion at the end of 2025." Also CFO Krishna Rao cited on Invest Like the Best podcast (~$30B, later ~$44-47B in May 2026 per Simon Willison's aggregation: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/29/anthropic/). For OpenAI: Sam Altman public statement (CNBC, Nov 6 2025: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/06/sam-altman-says-openai-will-top-20-billion-annual-revenue-this-year.html — "on track to top $20B annualized"); The Information's paywalled scoop "OpenAI Tops $25 Billion in Annualized Revenue as Anthropic Narrows Gap" (https://www.theinformation.com/articles/openai-tops-25-billion-annualized-revenue-anthropic-narrows-gap — confirmed paywalled, headline visible, body locked behind subscribe wall).
CADENCE: Irregular/opportunistic — tied to funding-round announcements, podcast appearances, or investigative leaks, not a recurring scheduled disclosure. Anthropic has disclosed updated figures roughly monthly during 2026's hypergrowth (Jan $14B → Mar $19B → Apr $30B → May $44-47B per aggregated reporting); OpenAI's disclosures are sparser and via press/interviews.
COST: Free for official company blog posts and most press coverage (CNBC, VentureBeat, Bloomberg); The Information (the outlet doing the most rigorous leaked-figure reporting) is paywalled, subscription required.
CAVEATS: Self-reported, unaudited (both are private companies, no SEC filings). "Run-rate" is a single month/quarter annualized, not GAAP revenue — highly sensitive to short-term spikes. Figures from different outlets conflict in the same window (OpenAI cited anywhere from $20B to $33B for similar periods). No consistent disclosure cadence or format between the two labs, so a scoreboard would be comparing apples picked at different times by different methods.
VERDICT: feedable with effort

METRIC 7: AI product revenue economy-wide (aggregate estimates)
SOURCE: Menlo Ventures "2025: State of Generative AI in the Enterprise" — https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/ (full PDF: https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/menlo_ventures_enterprise_ai_report-2025-123125.pdf) reports $37B enterprise gen-AI spend in 2025 vs $11.5B in 2024. Gartner press releases — https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-1-15-gartner-says-worldwide-ai-spending-will-total-2-point-5-trillion-dollars-in-2026 and the May 2026 update (https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-19-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-ai-spending-to-grow-47-percent-in-2026) citing $2.59T total AI spend. IDC AI infrastructure forecast (~$487B, cited secondhand, full report paywalled).
CADENCE: Menlo — annual (December). Gartner — revises forecasts multiple times per year (Jan and May 2026 releases already diverged: $2.52T vs $2.59T for the same year).
COST: Menlo report free (PDF download); Gartner/IDC press releases free but full underlying reports require paid subscriptions (Gartner research access, IDC report licenses run into the thousands of dollars).
CAVEATS: Menlo's methodology is explicitly survey-based (495 US enterprise decision-makers) plus qualitative market modeling — their own report states "Revenue estimates for private companies are based on public data and industry analysis," i.e. not measured/audited. Gartner and IDC numbers measure fundamentally different things (Gartner = total AI spending incl. hardware/hyperscaler capex; IDC = AI infrastructure only; Menlo = enterprise gen-AI application spend) and are not reconcilable into one series. No source offers a transparent, replicable formula — all are proprietary vendor methodologies.
VERDICT: feedable with effort

METRIC 8: METR autonomous task-completion time horizon
SOURCE: https://metr.org/time-horizons/ — live interactive dashboard. Originating paper/blog: https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/ (paper: arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499); update https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/ (task-suite revision, Jan 29 2026); raw data at GitHub METR/eval-analysis-public.
CADENCE: Not fixed — METR states on the page: "We intend to update this page periodically whenever we have new measurements to share." Confirmed update history spans March 2025 through May 8, 2026 (most recent addition: Claude Mythos Preview), with gaps from days to months. Coverage is not comprehensive — METR explicitly notes capacity limits mean some model releases get skipped entirely.
COST: Free.
CAVEATS: Single research org, not independently audited; task-suite methodology changed between v1.0 and v1.1, so cross-version comparisons need care; METR itself publishes caveat posts (e.g. "Clarifying limitations of time horizon," https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/) acknowledging measurement uncertainty; not every frontier model gets evaluated promptly (or at all).
VERDICT: feedable now

METRIC 13: Inference cost per token at fixed capability
SOURCE: Artificial Analysis — https://artificialanalysis.ai/ and https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/prices. Live comparison table (USD per M tokens: input/output/cache-hit/cache-write) across all major providers, plus a genuinely capability-normalized metric: "Cost per Intelligence Index Task," weighted by the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1 (a 9-benchmark composite). Raw inputs for cross-checking: openai.com/api/pricing, anthropic.com/pricing, ai.google.dev/pricing (official list prices).
CADENCE: Continuous — changelog shows near-daily entries (e.g. "10 Jul," "9 Jul" 2026 model additions); pricing figures use a "median (P50) measurement over the past 72 hours" to smooth short-term fluctuation.
COST: Free for the public site; Artificial Analysis also sells enterprise/API data products (pricing not disclosed on the free pages).
CAVEATS: The Intelligence Index composite is Artificial Analysis's own proprietary weighting, not a universally agreed capability measure — a different weighting could shift the "$/capability" ranking. Listed prices are list/API prices, not negotiated enterprise rates, which can diverge substantially at volume. Doesn't capture real-world workload token mix (heavy reasoning vs. light completion).
VERDICT: feedable now — this is the strongest source in the set for genuinely normalizing cost by capability rather than raw $/token.

METRIC 18: AI share of total US private investment / GDP
SOURCE: No official AI-specific line item exists. Closest official proxies: BEA GDP releases (bea.gov, quarterly) break out "information processing equipment" and "intellectual property products" (software/R&amp;D) investment categories, not AI specifically. FRED series for the equipment/software proxy: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/A679RC1Q027SBEA and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/A679RC1A027NBEA. BEA is actively researching AI-specific measurement via working papers, not yet a standing series: "Early Estimates of the Impact of AI Within BEA's Industry Economic Accounts" (https://www.bea.gov/research/papers/2026/early-estimates-impact-ai-within-beas-industry-economic-accounts, PDF: https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/papers/BEA-WP2026-3.pdf) and a companion 2025 paper "Concepts and Challenges of Measuring Production of Artificial Intelligence in the U.S. Economy" (https://www.bea.gov/research/papers/2025/concepts-and-challenges-measuring-production-artificial-intelligence-us). Private-sector estimates layering analyst math on top of the BEA equipment/software categories exist (e.g. https://www.tftc.io/ai-capex-gdp-growth-q1-2026-bea-third-estimate/ claiming AI capex drove ~74% of Q1 2026 GDP growth) but these are not official government output. Note: I did not find independent confirmation of a Torsten Slok/Apollo-specific note in this pass — flagging that as unverified rather than asserting it.
CADENCE: BEA GDP releases are quarterly (advance/second/third estimates) and FRED series update on the same schedule; BEA's AI-specific working papers appear occasionally/non-recurring (research output, not a standing statistical product) — BEA states it "plans to release... experimental statistics on the size of the American AI economy in 2026" but this was not yet a repeating published series as of the papers found.
COST: Free (BEA and FRED are public government data).
CAVEATS: The biggest caveat is definitional — "AI investment" as an official category does not exist in the national accounts; the info-processing-equipment/software proxy includes large amounts of non-AI IT spending, so it systematically overstates "AI" specifically. BEA's own working papers on this are explicitly labeled experimental/early-stage. Private analyst estimates of "AI's contribution to GDP growth" vary widely in methodology and are not a repeatable, citable time series in the way FRED data is.
VERDICT: no reliable public source (AI-specific); feedable with effort only as an acknowledged proxy (info-processing-equipment + software investment as % of GDP via FRED), with the caveat prominently flagged as non-AI-specific.

Overall note: This cluster (6, 7, 18) sits mostly at "feedable with effort" or worse because the underlying phenomena (private-company financials, aggregate market spend, and a not-yet-existent government AI category) genuinely lack authoritative, repeatable public series — any scoreboard feeding these needs a human/analyst layer to reconcile conflicting figures each update cycle. Metrics 8 (METR) and 13 (Artificial Analysis) are the strongest in the set — both are live, freely accessible, and updated by credible third parties on their own initiative, though neither is fully comprehensive (METR skips some model releases; Artificial Analysis's capability-normalization is a proprietary index, not a neutral standard).

METRIC 9: Algorithmic efficiency (compute for fixed capability, halving time)
SOURCE: Epoch AI, "Algorithmic progress in language models" — https://epoch.ai/blog/algorithmic-progress-in-language-models (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05812, published Mar 12, 2024). Ongoing related but separate research at https://epoch.ai/topics/software-progress (e.g. "Quantifying the algorithmic improvement from reasoning models," Aug 2, 2025; "The least understood driver of AI progress," Feb 25, 2026).
CADENCE: No live tracker or refreshable dataset. Topic page shows sporadic one-off papers/posts (2021–2026) on different sub-questions, not a recurring series measuring the same halving-time number over time. To "refresh" this metric someone would need to commission/wait for Epoch to redo the analysis.
COST: Free.
CAVEATS: Headline figure — compute-for-fixed-performance halves ~every 8 months (95% CI 5–14 months) — is based on 231 models (2012–2023) evaluated on Wikitext/Penn Treebank perplexity, covers pretraining gains only (excludes post-training/inference-time gains like reasoning models, RLHF), and the authors flag wide uncertainty in their own methodology.
VERDICT: feedable with effort (one credible historical number exists, but no mechanism refreshes it on a schedule — would need a new Epoch paper or an analyst to replicate the method).

METRIC 10: AI R&amp;D automation measures (RE-Bench and successors; % code written by AI)
SOURCE: METR RE-Bench — https://metr.org/blog/2024-11-22-evaluating-r-d-capabilities-of-llms/ and https://metr.org/AI_R_D_Evaluation_Report.pdf (Nov 22, 2024; arXiv 2411.15114). Successor/live-updated benchmark: METR Time Horizon — https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/ and https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-14-how-does-time-horizon-vary-across-domains/, visualized at https://theaidigest.org/time-horizons. Lab statements: Dario Amodei's "90% of code within 3-6 months" claim (Council on Foreign Relations, Mar 10, 2025) and Google/Sundar Pichai's public figures — 25% (Oct 2024 earnings call) → 50% ("last fall") → 75% (Apr 22, 2026 Cloud Next blog post).
CADENCE: RE-Bench itself was a single benchmark release (Nov 2024) — no evidence of a maintained leaderboard or repeat runs on newer models found via search. METR's Time Horizon work is the actively updated successor, with new posts roughly quarterly (Mar 2025, Jul 2025, Jan 2026 "TH1.1" update). Lab code-generation stats are irregular, self-timed PR/earnings-call disclosures (Google: Oct 2024, "last fall" ~2025, Apr 2026 — roughly every 6 months so far, but not a formal commitment).
COST: Free (METR reports and AI Digest tracker are public).
CAVEATS: RE-Bench has only 7 tasks and stale model coverage (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, o1-preview) — not repeatable/comparable to current frontier models without METR re-running it. Time Horizon doubling rate is itself contested/moving (7-month doubling 2019–2025, accelerating to ~4 months in 2024–2025, then ~3 months per a Jan 2026 METR update) — the "constant" is not constant. Lab-reported "% code written by AI" figures are self-reported, methodologically undefined (what counts as "AI-written" varies), unaudited, and disputed by third parties (e.g., Redwood Research's critique of Anthropic's 90% claim). No independent audit exists for either Anthropic's or Google's numbers.
VERDICT: feedable with effort — METR Time Horizon is the closest thing to a genuine repeatable, comparable metric; RE-Bench and lab self-reported %s are one-off/anecdotal and not reliably comparable over time.

METRIC 14: Model release cadence at the frontier (releases above compute thresholds)
SOURCE: Epoch AI Notable AI Models data explorer — https://epoch.ai/data/notable-ai-models (also https://epoch.ai/data/ai-models). Related analysis: https://epoch.ai/data-insights/models-over-1e25-flop and https://epoch.ai/blog/model-counts-compute-thresholds.
CADENCE: Actively maintained — per Epoch's own FAQ, "an automated search process identifies newly released models each week using the Google Search API," supplemented by manual review; major models added within ~2 weeks of release. This is a genuine live/refreshable dataset, not a one-off report.
COST: Free; underlying dataset downloadable.
CAVEATS: Dataset includes both "Training compute (FLOP)" and "Publication date" fields, so a release-cadence-above-threshold metric (e.g., count of models/month above 1e25 FLOP) is directly computable — Epoch has already published this analysis (33 models above 1e25 FLOP as of their count, ~2/month in 2024). Compute estimates for many models are themselves Epoch estimates, not lab-disclosed figures, and "notability" inclusion criteria (SOTA, citations, cost &gt;$1M, users) introduce selection judgment. Regulatory angle: EU AI Act presumes systemic risk at ≥10^25 FLOP and requires provider notification to the EU AI Office within 2 weeks of reaching that threshold — but this notification goes through a confidential "SEND" platform; no public register of notified models was found. The pre-2025 US reporting requirement (EO 14110, Defense Production Act reporting to Commerce) was rescinded in Jan 2025 by Trump's EO; a Jun 2, 2026 EO establishes only a voluntary pre-release review framework, not a mandatory disclosure list. So no regulatory source adds public disclosure beyond what Epoch already tracks independently.
VERDICT: feedable now (Epoch's dataset alone supports this metric on a live basis).

METRIC 15: Frontier-model training cost estimates
SOURCE: Epoch AI, "How much does it cost to train frontier AI models?" — https://epoch.ai/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-train-frontier-ai-models (Jun 3, 2024; paper arXiv 2405.21015, corrected Jan 2025). Related: https://epoch.ai/blog/power-demands-of-frontier-ai-training, https://epoch.ai/data-insights/openai-compute-spend (Oct 2025). Also indexed at Our World in Data: https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/hardware-and-energy-cost-to-train-notable-ai-systems (pulls from Epoch's dataset).
CADENCE: Not a live tracker — periodic research posts (Jun 2024 paper, Jun 2024 data insight on doubling costs, Jan 2025 correction, Oct 2025 spend breakdown) rather than a continuously updated per-model cost series. Our World in Data's grapher is refreshed whenever Epoch updates its underlying dataset, so it's the closest thing to a "live" front end but still depends on Epoch's irregular update cycle.
COST: Free.
CAVEATS: These are estimates, not disclosed costs — modeled from chip-hours × depreciated hardware cost, cloud rental-rate proxies, and R&amp;D staff cost assumptions (three separate estimation methods that can disagree by ~2x). No frontier lab (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) publicly discloses actual training run cost; GPT-4 (~$40M) and Gemini Ultra (~$30M) are Epoch's estimates, not company-confirmed figures. Cited growth rate: 2.4x/year (95% CI 2.0–3.1x) hardware+energy cost since 2016, ~2.6x/year via the cloud-rental method.
VERDICT: feedable with effort (credible, reasoned estimates exist and are occasionally updated, but there is no audited/disclosed ground truth and no fixed refresh cadence).

Stanford HAI AI Index Report
SOURCE: https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index (redirects from https://aiindex.stanford.edu/report/). Annual report, 2026 edition is current.
COVERAGE OF METRICS 9/10/14/15: Fetched the current landing page directly and could not confirm explicit sections on algorithmic efficiency, AI R&amp;D automation/coding stats, frontier release cadence, or training-cost estimates from the page content alone — the landing page covers technical capability trends, adoption, economic impact, and public opinion at a high level. CADENCE: Confirmed annual.

Overall note on this cluster's data quality: Metric 14 (release cadence) is the strongest — a genuinely live, weekly-refreshed dataset with the needed fields. # Measurability Audit — Metrics 11, 12, 16, 17

**METRIC 11: GPU production/shipment volumes**
SOURCE: NVIDIA 10-Q/10-K filings on SEC EDGAR, e.g. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000230/nvda-20251026.htm (Q3 FY2026, filed) and press release https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2026 (Data Center revenue $41.1B Q2 FY2026, +56% YoY). Also TSMC investor materials/earnings calls (CapEx guidance $52–56B for 2026, ~10–20% to advanced packaging) and TrendForce free news bulletins, e.g. https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/06/15/news-tsmc-cowos-supply-demand-gap-reportedly-seen-narrowing-from-20-to-10-by-end-2026-as-capacity-expands/ (CoWoS capacity target ~125–130k wafers/month by end-2026).
CADENCE: Quarterly (NVIDIA 10-Q, TSMC earnings); TrendForce news bulletins ~weekly/monthly.
COST: NVIDIA/TSMC filings free (SEC EDGAR, investor relations). TrendForce free press bulletins exist, but the underlying full HBM Industry Analysis / DRAMeXchange reports are paywalled — TrendForce directs buyers to contact sales (SR_MI@trendforce.com); no public price was found, so treat as "contact for quote."
CAVEATS: I confirmed via WebFetch on the SEC filing search results that "Data Center" is disclosed as a **revenue-by-market-platform** line (ASC 606 disaggregation), not as NVIDIA's formal ASC 280 reportable segment — the actual GAAP segments are "Compute &amp; Networking" and "Graphics." So it's real, audited, quarterly-disclosed data, but calling it a "segment" is technically imprecise. TSMC doesn't disclose exact GPU/HBM unit shipment counts — only revenue, CapEx %, and qualitative capacity targets (frequently revised, sourced to management commentary rather than a fixed table). HBM unit-level supply data (SK Hynix/Samsung/Micron market share) exists mainly via TrendForce paid reports; free TrendForce news gives share % but not full time series. None of this yields a clean "GPU units produced" number — it's all revenue/capacity proxies.
VERDICT: feedable with effort (NVIDIA Data Center $ revenue is feedable now; HBM/TSMC volume data requires paywalled reports or accepting rough estimates).

**METRIC 12: GPU cloud rental prices and utilization**
SOURCE: Vast.ai marketplace pricing — https://vast.ai/pricing and per-GPU pages e.g. https://vast.ai/pricing/gpu/H100-SXM ; RunPod — https://www.runpod.io/pricing ; Lambda Labs — https://lambda.ai/pricing ; SemiAnalysis ClusterMAX rating system — https://www.clustermax.ai/ (and criteria page https://www.clustermax.ai/criteria).
CADENCE: Vast.ai/RunPod/Lambda list prices update continuously/live (marketplace-driven for Vast.ai, static list for RunPod/Lambda). ClusterMAX is versioned/periodic (2.0 → 2.1 → 3.0 in progress, roughly every few months to a year).
COST: All free to view publicly.
CAVEATS: I directly WebFetched vast.ai/pricing and vast.ai/pricing/gpu/H100-SXM twice — both times the actual dollar figures did not appear in the fetched HTML/text (they're injected client-side via JavaScript into a "Live GPU Prices" widget); a plain HTTP fetch/scraper will not reliably pull the number, only a browser-rendering approach or their (undocumented) backend API would work. RunPod and Lambda list prices are static/on-demand rack-rate quotes, not the actual clearing price paid by large buyers (which are individually negotiated and confidential). **Utilization rates are not published anywhere I could find** — ClusterMAX rates qualitative dimensions (reliability, networking, support) on a tier scale, not a numeric utilization %, and no provider or aggregator discloses fleet utilization; this is treated as proprietary/internal across the industry.
VERDICT: feedable with effort for prices (needs a JS-capable scraper or reliance on RunPod/Lambda static pages instead of Vast.ai); no reliable public source for utilization.

**METRIC 16: Chip export-control scope changes / policy events**
SOURCE: Federal Register REST API — https://www.federalregister.gov/developers/documentation/api/v1 ; example verified document: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00789/revision-to-license-review-policy-for-advanced-computing-commodities (JSON at https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-00789.json). BIS rules/press page: https://www.bis.gov/regulations. CSIS commentary/analysis: https://www.csis.org/analysis/updated-october-7-semiconductor-export-controls (context, not a structured feed).
CADENCE: Event-driven — new rule whenever BIS publishes one (several times per year); API is queryable anytime.
COST: Free, no API key required.
CAVEATS: I successfully pulled the JSON for a real document via WebFetch and confirmed exact fields: title "Revision to License Review Policy for Advanced Computing Commodities," publication_date and effective date both 2026-01-15, agency = Bureau of Industry and Security (Dept. of Commerce), plus an abstract describing the H200/MI325X-class license-review shift from "presumption of denial" to "case-by-case review." This is genuinely a structured, dated, machine-readable event log (title, date, agency, docket number, full text link) — the strongest source among all four metrics. CSIS/CSET are useful for qualitative interpretation of *why* a rule matters but are not themselves structured trackers — direct WebFetch of federalregister.gov's HTML pages was blocked by anti-scraping (403/CAPTCHA redirect), so any automated pipeline must use the official API, not page scraping.
VERDICT: feedable now.

**METRIC 17: Sovereign/national AI compute programs**
SOURCE: No single aggregator with $ + GW figures found. Individual official sources: EU — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-factories and https://commission.europa.eu/topics/competitiveness/competitiveness-coordination-tool-projects/ai-gigafactories_en (€20B InvestAI facility, up to 5 gigafactories, 77 proposals from 16 member states); UK — https://www.sovereignai.gov.uk/ and AI Growth Zones coverage (5 zones, £28.2B claimed private investment); nearest thing to an aggregator: OECD.AI's compute-geography project — https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/the-geopgraphy-of-ai-compute-mapping-what-is-available-and-where — and Epoch AI's Frontier Data Centers Hub — https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-the-frontier-data-centers-hub/.
CADENCE: Individual government/EC announcements are irregular/one-off; OECD.AI pilot dataset was a 2023 snapshot with stated plans to expand; Epoch AI hub cadence not specified but framed as an ongoing/growing database.
COST: All free.
CAVEATS: I WebFetched the OECD.AI page directly and confirmed it explicitly does **not** track sovereign programs' dollars or GW — it maps commercial cloud-region chip availability across ~187 regions/39 economies (2023 pilot) and explicitly states it shows "availability... but not the quantity." Epoch AI's Frontier Data Centers Hub tracks individual large data-center facilities (power, compute, construction timelines via satellite/permit data, CC-BY licensed) but isn't organized around "national program" totals and (per the announcement) started with 13 US facilities before expanding globally — it wasn't clear it separates sovereign-government-funded facilities from private hyperscaler ones. So building this metric requires manually aggregating individual government/EC press releases (each citable and free, but inconsistent in units — some report $ committed, some report private investment claims, some report GW, some report chip counts — e.g. UK's £28.2B private investment figure vs. EU's €20B public seed figure are not apples-to-apples) and cross-checking physical build-out against Epoch AI's facility-level data where available.
VERDICT: no reliable single public source (feedable only via manual aggregation of individual government announcements, each individually verifiable but not indexed anywhere).

**Overall note:** This cluster splits sharply on measurability. Metric 16 (export-control events) is the cleanest — a genuine government API with dated, structured records. Metric 11 is usable but requires accepting revenue/capacity proxies rather than true unit-volume data, with the best free component (NVIDIA's dollar figure) not technically a GAAP segment. Metrics 12 and 17 both suffer from the same underlying problem: the numbers exist on the public web but not in a stable, structured, single-fetch form — Metric 12's real-time prices are JS-rendered marketplace data requiring a scraper (and utilization is genuinely undisclosed anywhere), while Metric 17 has no aggregator at all and must be manually stitched from incommensurable national announcements.
